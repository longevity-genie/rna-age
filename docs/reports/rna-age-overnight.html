Motivation

Can a bulk-transcriptome foundation model read a donor's age from RNA-seq better than the classic linear clocks, across tissues, studies and even single-cell pseudobulk?

On 2026-11-04 the BulkFormer authors (Kang et al., Cell Systems 2026) released two models: BulkFormer-Atlas (242 M parameters, a 1,024-d CLS sample embedding from a fixed 4,096-gene panel; Atlas + HVG ranks first on the PRIMO patient-level bulk benchmark) and BulkFormer-Latest (154 M parameters, 768-d contextual gene embeddings over the full 19,973-gene coding transcriptome). Age is the textbook sample-level phenotype: every public RNA-seq sample with a donor age is a free label, and a good transcriptomic clock is useful for aging research, for disease "age acceleration", and as a stress test of how much biology a foundation model captures beyond batch and tissue.

Tonight's goal: build the data side (reproducible downloaders and parsers, a harmonised sample table with raw counts and donor ages), fix an evaluation protocol before any model, then climb a training ladder: linear baselines, frozen Atlas embeddings with probes, Atlas fine-tuning, and compare everything with published clocks (tAge, ClockBase) on the same held-out samples.

Methods

Pipeline

rna-age fetch (resumable, parallel HTTP ranges, checksums) → rna-age parse (per-source samples.parquet + raw counts as gene × sample parquet parts) → rna-age merge (dedup, cohort labels, splits) → models. Everything is polars + parquet; heavy steps run as batch jobs.

Evaluation protocol (fixed before the first model)

  • Whole studies (GSE, Census dataset, Bgee experiment; GTEx donor) go to train / val / test (70/15/15) by a stable hash: no series or donor spans two splits. 99,559 samples carry a split.
  • Training set: bulk train split, healthy or unknown health status, exact ages; GTEx bins enter as midpoints in baselines and as an interval loss in fine-tuning.
  • Metrics: MAE, median AE, Pearson r, R², bias slope (regression to the mean); GTEx scored as interval hit rate and distance to the bin; per source, tissue, decade, health status and modality.
  • Transfer test: every pseudobulk sample predicted by models trained on bulk only.
  • Controls: tAge (relative clock, compared on age deltas within dataset × tissue strata) on the same samples, with and without the series it was trained on.

Training ladder

  1. Ridge / ElasticNet on log-CPM of highly variable genes chosen on train only (2k–20k genes; optional Horvath log-linear age target).
  2. BulkFormer-Atlas frozen CLS embeddings (fixed 4,096-gene panel chosen on train, fp16 on RTX 2080 Ti), ridge and MLP probes, CLS + HVG.
  3. Atlas fine-tuning: last N encoder blocks + regression head, Huber loss on exact ages + interval loss on GTEx bins, per-study sampling cap, early stopping on validation MAE.

tAge outputs are relative (centred per dataset × tissue), so all models are scored by the correlation of their within-stratum deviation with the true within-stratum age deviation. Our ridge saw other GTEx donors in training; tAge never saw GTEx.

What drives the ridge clock

Strongest "older" genes: EYA4, PTCHD4, NAP1L2, LMO3, ENSG00000244306, ZNF727, MIR34AHG, IGFBP3, CPS1, FIGN, DDO, NEFL. Strongest "younger" genes: RANBP17, ZNF518B, NEFH, HCG11, PODXL2, NRCAM, NKAPL, PIANP, SLC4A10, KLRB1, CD248, KCNG1. Known aging genes rank high (PTCHD4, LRRN3, CD248, the miR-34a and miR-29 host genes).

Problems & decisions

  • NCBI started answering its GEO download endpoint with a reCAPTCHA page: NCBI-generated counts are parked; we do not work around bot protection.
  • Synapse token on the cluster answered 401 (also with the official client): Terekhova counts came from the HCA copy, with raw UMIs recovered from log1p-CP10k (verified integral).
  • CELLxGENE Census SOMA reads stalled through the proxy: we used the same LTS release's h5ads over HTTPS; four chosen datasets were not in that release and were skipped to keep the release pinned.
  • ARCHS4 extraction ran out of memory twice (a 60 GB copy into a memory-backed temp dir; h5py fancy indexing over scattered columns): replaced by bounded dense windows, 75k samples in 4.5 minutes.
  • GPUs are 11 GB cards without native bf16: fp16 autocast (checked against fp32, cosine 1.000); Atlas passed an all-true attention mask that forced the full-matrix attention kernel, patched at runtime with identical maths.
  • Training filter bug caught before the first result: "healthy or unknown" silently dropped unknowns (polars null semantics), which would have cut training to 5,208 samples.
  • Choices made on Anton's behalf: GTEx bins as midpoints for baselines / interval loss for fine-tuning; healthy-or-unknown training set; per-study sampling cap; pseudobulk only as a transfer test; tAge compared on within-stratum deltas.
  • A 54 MB R diagnostics PDF slipped into three commits before it was ignored; history was not rewritten.
